1. Primary objective
Find out when a news search engine benefits from semantic matching and when exact wording still matters. We
compare BM25 with E5 dense retrieval over the same articles, focusing on vocabulary mismatch, negation,
ambiguous words and event dates. The aim is a working comparison demo and an evidence-based account of
their strengths and failures. E5 is a neural bi-encoder; the findings concern this model and setup, rather than all
LLMs.
2. Dataset and evaluation criteria
Selected dataset: TREC-News mirror — liuqi6777/trec-news. The downloaded corpus has 594,977 records.
Excluding 388 records with no title or body leaves 594,589 articles for both engines. Title-only articles
remain in the baseline so the two systems search the same IDs.
We evaluate two distinct tasks. The 57 original background-linking topics have 15,655 supplied
relevance judgments. Our 40 custom queries have ten questions per category: vocabulary mismatch,
negation, word sense and temporal constraints. The original 20 are preserved; 20 follow-up questions add
ransomware, bail reform, Java/Mercury senses and dated disaster responses. Wording, inclusion rules and
event windows were frozen before follow-up retrieval, after viewing the initial results. This is an exploratory
extension, not an independent held-out replication.
Success measures: nDCG@5 measures how well the first five results are ordered by relevance; MAP@1000
measures recovery and ordering of known relevant articles across the saved top 1,000. We also report
Precision@5, category means, judged coverage and paired query-bootstrap intervals. AP divides by all known
positives for the query. Main nDCG gains equal relevance grades: 0–4 for supplied judgments and 0–2 for the
custom study.
Assessment scope and next validation: the current comparison uses thorough, full-article AI judgments
for all 1,444 query–article pairs in the equal-depth top-20 union: 737 preserved initial decisions and 707
added decisions. Each judgment follows a frozen 0–2 rubric and has a rationale, source hash and quoted
evidence. Human review is planned next, beginning with balanced cases across all 40 queries and
adjudication of disagreements; it has not yet been completed. The custom results are therefore preliminary.
Grade 0 fails a mandatory constraint, 1 gives limited qualifying detail, and 2 gives substantive support. Every
top-20 hit is assessed, but only about 3% of each top-1,000 list is judged, so custom MAP is a shallow-pool
estimate. Unjudged hits keep their ranks with zero contribution; all 40 queries remain in the averages,
including V05 with no pooled positive.

3. Related research: approach, findings and relevance
Thakur et al. (2021), BEIR: A Heterogenous Benchmark for Zero-shot Evaluation of Information
Retrieval Models. The paper evaluates ten retrieval systems across 18 datasets. BM25 is a robust baseline;
reranking and late-interaction methods have strong average quality at greater computational cost. This supports
keeping BM25 as a serious comparator and examining different query types.
Wang et al. (2022; revised 2024), Text Embeddings by Weakly-Supervised Contrastive Pre-
training. E5 learns text embeddings through contrastive training on text pairs and reports strong transfer on
BEIR/MTEB tasks. We use a released E5 checkpoint to test whether this semantic representation helps with
news queries whose wording differs from relevant articles.
Weller, Lawrie and Van Durme (2024), NevIR: Negation in Neural Information Retrieval. The
authors compare documents differing in negation. Many retrievers struggle with polarity; cross-encoders
perform better than bi-encoders. This motivates explicit negation tests instead of assuming that semantic
similarity enforces a negative constraint.
Cormack, Clarke and Büttcher (2009), Reciprocal Rank Fusion outperforms Condorcet and
individual Rank Learning Methods. The paper combines ranked lists through reciprocal-rank
contributions and reports gains over individual systems in its experiments. We test fixed RRF with k=60 as an
extension, without fitting weights to our judgments.

Component Method and reason for the choice
BM25 Lucene inverted index; k1=0.9, b=0.4. A strong term-based baseline makes exact-name and wording
effects visible.
Dense retrieval E5-base-v2: query:/passage: prefixes, mean pooling, normalized 768-D vectors; exact FAISS cosine
ranking. Tests semantic matching without approximate-index error.
Hybrid and
inspection
Fixed equal-weight RRF, sum(1/(60+rank)); no tuning. Article lookup and the demo connect ranked
IDs to full text and relevance evidence.
Both engines use title plus body. English lexical analysis lowercases and tokenizes, preserves stopwords and
does not stem. BM25 reads full bodies; E5 retains the first 512 tokens, including its prefix and special tokens.
This difference is audited and remains a limitation of the comparison. BM25 treats “NOT” as a term, with no
Boolean negation or date filter. The lexical foundations follow Manning et al., Introduction to Information
Retrieval, Chapters 6 and 11.

The larger suite changes the comparison. E5 leads the initial 20, while BM25 leads the added 20 and the
combined 40. BM25 wins 21 queries at nDCG@5, E5 wins 15, and four tie. The combined vocabulary-mismatch
result also reverses the initial pattern. More queries expose topic and wording sensitivity; they do not guarantee
the expected neural advantage. RRF remains an original-benchmark extension: nDCG@5 0.4900, MAP
0.3332.
Failure modes: exact entities and detailed constraints can help BM25. E5 sometimes retrieves the broad topic
while missing a required cause, response or date. In the word-sense category, BM25 has slightly higher
nDCG@5 but E5 higher pooled MAP; early ranking and recovery measure different behavior. Neither ranker
enforces negation or time as a logical constraint. Temporal exclusions include missing date evidence, not only
known out-of-window events.
Uncertainty: the combined E5−BM25 nDCG difference is −0.0771, with a descriptive 95% family-bootstrap
interval [-0.1729, +0.0150] (37 topic families; 10,000 draws). Query and broader 35-family analyses also
include zero for nDCG and MAP. These intervals do not measure label accuracy or establish a general winner.
A practical improvement: on the preserved initial 20, removing empty-body candidates raises E5 nDCG@5
0.4897 → 0.5492, P@5 0.53 → 0.60, with BM25 unchanged. This bounded, post-hoc top-20 ablation has
not been rerun on the added queries.
Resources and checks: BM25 index 367.6 MiB, dense index 1.72 GiB, model about 418 MiB on disk.
BM25 built in 66.55 s locally; T4 encoding took 5 h 43 min. One cold CPU query took 1.4/48.6 s (BM25/E5),
including startup; hardware/stages differ. Peak RAM and controlled warm latency remain unmeasured.
Verification: 153 passing tests, 7,196 native metric comparisons, and verified saved demo views; no new
GPU encoding was required.

6. Key innovations and stretch objectives
The project adds a constraint-focused evaluation and transparent inspection tools to established rankers. Three
additions make it useful: a query suite with explicit linguistic constraints; evidence-linked judgments that
explain why an article qualifies; and an input-visibility audit showing what text E5 retained. A symmetric
quality experiment measures how article quality affects the rankings.
Completed stretch objectives: fixed RRF on the original 57 topics; exact-content deduplication and empty-
body filtering within judged candidates; gain/confidence sensitivity; and a full-article demo with an E5 input
toggle. Further work: complete the planned human-review stage, then test controlled polarity pairs, equal-
input or passage-based retrieval, and a cross-encoder reranker. These extensions need fresh validation; no
results for them are claimed.
Expanded evaluation completed: 40 queries, ten per category; all 1,444 designated pairs assessed. Initial,
added and combined results are retained separately, with topic-family uncertainty and full-text evidence.
